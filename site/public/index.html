Make a machine your cloud

A Raspberry Pi or a PC with Linux, at home. On it:

$curl -fsSL https://tpcloud.migueltaibo.com/install | sh

It asks how you want to set it up: right there, or from the app or a browser at http://tpcloud.local. Its tpcloud does everything, server and client. On NixOS it gives you the lines for configuration.nix first.

Your files, your photos and your house, on a machine at home.

Instead of somebody else’s servers. One sign-in for all of it, at your own address, from the browser or the TPCloud app.

/store · /photos

Files and photos

A file browser for everyone at home, the iPhone’s photos backed up in the background, and share links with or without a password.

/home

The house

Blinds and lights on a floor plan, with switches running tphome’s own firmware. And from the terminal: tphome --blind B0301 up.

/login

One sign-in

Passkeys on the web, password and two-factor in the app, and every session listed so you can close the ones you don’t know.

How it works

Set up your cloud

On a Raspberry Pi or a PC with Linux: one command, then a few questions in the terminal, the app or a browser. Files go in a folder or on disks it mirrors.

Get the apps

On your Mac, iPhone and Linux computers. They find your cloud on your Wi-Fi by themselves, and use your own address when you’re out.

Sign in

Everyone at home with their own account. Admins run it from anywhere: accounts, sessions and updates, from the apps, the web or tpcloud.
