Get TPCloud Get the app

Two things: the machine that is your cloud, and the devices you use it from.

1

The machine that is your cloud

A Raspberry Pi or a PC with Linux, at home. On it, run:

$curl -fsSL https://tpcloud.migueltaibo.com/install | sh

It picks the file for that machine and asks how you want to set it up: right there, or from the app or a browser at http://tpcloud.local.

Or the file itself: Raspberry Pi arm64 · 71 MB PC amd64 · 80 MB

Then sudo ./tpcloud-linux-<arch> install. On NixOS, the installer gives you the lines for configuration.nix first.

2

Your devices

Sign in to your cloud from any of them. They find it on your Wi-Fi by themselves.

Your files, your photos and your house, on a machine at home.

Instead of somebody else’s servers. One sign-in for all of it, at your own address, from the browser or the TPCloud app.

/store · /photos

Files and photos

A file browser for everyone at home, the iPhone’s photos backed up in the background, and share links with or without a password.

/home

The house

Blinds and lights on a floor plan, with switches running tphome’s own firmware. And from the terminal: tphome --blind B0301 up.

/login

One sign-in

Passkeys on the web, password and two-factor in the app, and every session listed so you can close the ones you don’t know.

Three steps, on a Raspberry Pi or an old PC.

Run the command

On the machine itself: curl -fsSL https://tpcloud.migueltaibo.com/install | sh. It picks the file for that machine.

Answer a few questions

In the terminal, the app or a browser: where files go (a folder, or empty disks it makes a mirror of), whether you want the house, how you’ll reach it (at home, Tailscale, your own domain or all of them) and your account.

Open it

From the apps or a browser, at tpcloud.local at home or your own address. On the server, tpcloud shows what’s running, accounts, disks and logs.
